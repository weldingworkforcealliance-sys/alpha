WLD 205 Day 4 — Instructor Delivery Guide

Objective: Move accurately between common fraction and decimal dimensions, perform decimal shop arithmetic, compare measurements, and round only when the required precision permits.

Before Class

Open the Day 4 Live Activity and student pack. Stage ruler/tape and caliper if available. Keep the live whiteboard visible. No tolerance accept/reject decision is made without a stated tolerance/source.

0–8 min — Fraction/Decimal Retrieval

Use 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8, 3/4, 7/8. Students complete Live Questions 1–7.

Correction cue: ask “What operation converts a fraction to a decimal?” Expected: numerator ÷ denominator.

Key: .125, .250, .375, .500, .625, .750, .875.

8–20 min — Instructor Model

3/8 in
3 ÷ 8 = 0.375 in
1 7/16 in
7 ÷ 16 = 0.4375; 1 + 0.4375 = 1.4375 in

Reverse reasoning: 0.625 = 625/1000 = 5/8 after simplification, but for common shop fractions students may use the authorized conversion reference.

Keep the unit attached. The number changing format does not change the physical length.

20–40 min — Decimal Shop Set A

Students solve and then answer Live Questions 8–11.

Key: 4.000 in; 4.3125 in; 3.75 in; 2.5 in.

Require work and one quick reasonableness statement. Correct the operation error only, then have the student redo the item.

40–54 min — Measurement Record

Print: 1 3/8 in = 1.375 in. Device: 1.372 in. Absolute difference = 0.003 in. Students complete Questions 12–13.

Ask: “Does a 0.003 difference automatically mean pass or fail?” Correct answer: no. A stated tolerance/source is needed.

54–60 min — Rounding/Tolerance Preview

Question 14: 2.3764 to nearest .001 = 2.376. Question 15: 4.000 ± .020 gives 3.980 to 4.020.

Day 4 previews tolerance logic. Day 7 owns formal accept/reject reasoning.

Focused Remediation

Classify the gap as one of: Fraction→Decimal | Decimal Operations | Measurement Comparison | Rounding/Precision | Tolerance Preview. Reteach only that domain with one parallel item, then require an independent recheck.

Do not: round a measured value to make it pass; invent a tolerance; treat visual closeness as acceptance.
