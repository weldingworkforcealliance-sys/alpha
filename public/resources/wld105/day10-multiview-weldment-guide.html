WLD 105 — Day 10 Multi-View Weldment Print + Interpretation Guide

40-minute blueprint focus: Read multiple views, notes, dimensions, tolerance, weld information, and part relationships as one coordinated fabrication document.

Controlling-source rule: This training print supports classroom practice. The school-authorized AWS A2.4 reference, assigned text, WPS/SWPS, and instructor-issued production print control when a conflict exists.

1. Complete Day 10 Training Print

Use the print below for the entire Day 10 blueprint block and the connected blueprint check. Zoom horizontally as needed. Do not answer from memory or from one view alone.

2. Read the Print in a Fixed Order

Step 1 — Identify the job

  1. Read the title block and units.
  2. Read material notes.
  3. Identify the two fabricated parts.
  4. Locate the general tolerance.

Step 2 — Coordinate the views

  1. Use the front view for overall width and plate height.
  2. Use the top view for base depth, hole locations, and plate position.
  3. Use the right-side view to confirm the plate is centered on the base depth.
  4. Transfer features across aligned views before making a layout decision.

Step 3 — Read fabrication requirements

  1. Read the 2X hole callout.
  2. Read the weld size, side, and length from the weld symbol.
  3. Check deburr and fit-up notes.
  4. Identify what must be verified before welding.

Step 4 — Convert to shop actions

  1. List material and finished sizes.
  2. Locate holes from stated dimensions, not appearance.
  3. Center the vertical plate using the coordinated views.
  4. Measure, calculate, check, and record before committing material.

3. Student Evidence Prompts

Students should point to the exact print evidence for each answer.

  • What gets made, and how many separate pieces are required before welding?
  • What material is specified?
  • What are the finished dimensions of the base plate?
  • What are the finished dimensions of the vertical plate?
  • How many holes are required, what diameter are they, and which part receives them?
  • Where are the hole centers located from the ends and front edge?
  • How is the vertical plate positioned on the base?
  • What weld size and weld length are specified?
  • Which tolerance controls dimensions without a more specific tolerance?
  • What must be checked before welding begins?

4. Instructor Flow — 40 Minutes

0–7 min

Retrieval: students identify the title block, material, three views, general tolerance, and weld symbol. Require pointing, not guessing.

7–16 min

Model one complete read-through from title block to fabrication requirement. Show how a feature is confirmed across more than one view.

16–24 min

Students answer: What gets made? From what? To what size? Where are holes and welds? What must be checked?

24–33 min

Connect the print to layout, fit-up, cut-list thinking, tolerance, and inspection. Stop any assumption that is not supported by visible print evidence.

33–37 min

Reinforce MEASURE → CALCULATE → CHECK → RECORD using one dimension or hole-location decision from the print.

37–40 min

Launch the connected blueprint check. Keep the print available as the controlling classroom reference.

5. Common Misreads to Catch

  • Reading only the front view and missing hole-location information in the top view.
  • Treating the general tolerance as if it overrides a specifically stated tolerance.
  • Reading the weld symbol without checking which joint the arrow identifies.
  • Assuming the vertical plate position from appearance instead of confirming centering in the top and right-side views.
  • Ignoring inspection and fit-up notes because they are not dimensions.
