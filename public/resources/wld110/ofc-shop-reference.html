WLD 110 — Oxy-Fuel Cutting Shop Reference

This LTG reference supports the WLD 110 shop sequence. It does not replace PCCC procedures, the equipment manufacturer manual, instructor direction, or required PPE.

Stop-work rule: if a cylinder, regulator, hose, fitting, torch, tip, connection, flashback/backflow device, or work area appears damaged or unsafe, stop and notify the instructor. Do not invent pressure settings or repair gas equipment in class.

Pre-use gate

Cylinders & regulators
Secure cylinders upright as required. Confirm identification, condition, valve/regulator compatibility, and that oxygen equipment is free of oil/grease.
Hoses, torch & tip
Inspect for damage, leaks, worn connections, damaged seating surfaces, and the correct approved tip. Keep hoses out of traffic and hot-metal paths.
Work area
Ventilation, screens, PPE, fire prevention, cutting lane, stable work support, and hot-metal controls must be in place before lighting.
Operation
Use the PCCC/manufacturer-approved lighting, adjustment, cutting, and shutdown sequence. Instructor authorization controls actual operating settings.

Cut-quality check

When a cut is poor, separate equipment condition, approved settings, tip condition, travel speed, torch angle/height, and material condition. Change only one instructor-approved variable at a time, then inspect again.

Day-by-day WLD 110 focus

Day 1Pre-use inspection, cylinder security, hose/torch condition, leak awareness, and instructor-authorized setup.
Day 2External equipment condition checks. Tag damaged regulators, valves, hoses, torches, or fittings instead of improvising repairs.
Day 3Hot-work zone control: cylinder position, ventilation, screens, torch direction, and hot-metal boundaries.
Day 4OFC fundamentals. Follow PCCC/manufacturer procedures for tip selection, pressures, valves, lighting, and shutdown.
Day 5Fuel-gas and oxygen identification; keep oxygen equipment free of oil/grease and verify approved components.
Day 6Straight-cut practice: stable stance, clear cutting lane, correct travel, slag direction, and hot-metal control.
Day 7Shutdown checkpoint: close down using the approved sequence, bleed safely, release regulator tension as directed, and leave no hot work unattended.
Day 8Repeat the full inspection → authorization → operation → shutdown sequence without shortcuts.
Day 9Workstation organization: route hoses safely, keep walking paths clear, and protect equipment from sparks and traffic.
Day 10Secure work before cutting; confirm reference edge, square checks, hand position, and cutting path.
Day 11Cut-quality troubleshooting: change only instructor-approved variables and separate equipment faults from technique faults.
Day 12Performance checkpoint: demonstrate the complete safe OFC routine even during short evaluation cuts.
Day 13Full supervised OFC operation with controlled torch direction, stable body position, and complete shutdown.
Day 14Use the approved Fabricated M cut list; verify material, cut side, clamping, and hot-metal location before each cut.
Day 15Make equipment safe before troubleshooting. Inspect the cut, identify the likely cause, then change one approved variable at a time.
Day 16Fabricated M completion: maintain cutting-lane discipline, hot-metal identification, and safe grinder/cut transitions.
Day 17Repeated-part cutting: secure stock, identify hot pieces, and use explicit handoffs between layout, cutting, and inspection.
Day 18Project cutting: protect drilling/cutting zones, deburr safely, and keep finished and hot parts separated.
Day 19Correction work: do not rush recuts. Reconfirm the approved print/cut list and re-mark the hot-work zone.
Day 20Final OFC performance evidence: full inspection, authorized setup, controlled cut, shutdown, cleanup, and documentation.
Day 21Steel Dice support: use only the approved project print/cut list and keep falling sparks/slag away from people and equipment.
Day 22Steel Dice fit-up support: verify fixture stability and keep cutting/grinding activity separated from overhead welding work.
Day 23Final OFC evidence and shop closeout: complete the safe sequence, account for equipment, identify remaining hot metal, and store equipment correctly.
