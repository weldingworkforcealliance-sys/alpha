**Protected outcomes (verbatim from supplied syllabus):**

1. CLO 1: Perform safety inspections for GMAW, FCAW, and GTAW equipment.
2. CLO 2: Correctly set up GMAW, FCAW, and GTAW power sources, guns, torches, and gas systems.
3. CLO 3: Produce GMAW stringer beads and fillet welds in 1F–4F positions.
4. CLO 4: Produce FCAW stringer beads and fillet welds following proper parameter control.
5. CLO 5: Perform GTAW autogenous welds and filler-metal welds on ferrous and non-ferrous metals.
6. CLO 6: Interpret weld quality indicators and apply corrective adjustments to improve technique.

### Days 1–8 — GTAW

| Day | Objective and demonstration | Guided/independent shop application | Evidence and coaching | CLOs; instructor slides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Inspect GTAW stations and gas cylinders; identify nonconsumable tungsten, torch components, shielding, emergency stops, and the authorized setup sequence. | Instructor checks every station before energizing. Students dry-run torch position and safe shutdown, then establish short, controlled autogenous arcs on clean steel. | Signed safety/setup checklist; label one baseline coupon. Coach arc length and hand support before speed. | 1, 2, 5; GTAW 4–16, 23–44, 94–100 |
| 2 | Demonstrate tungsten selection/preparation, contamination recognition, and clean material preparation. | Prepare approved tungsten and steel coupons; repeat autogenous beads with consistent torch angle and travel. Stop and correct contaminated tips. | Record settings and one before/after bead; explain why a contaminated tip must be corrected. | 1, 2, 5, 6; GTAW 46–64, 83–88, 91–93 |
| 3 | Demonstrate current/polarity selection for the approved material, arc initiation, and heat control. | Run autogenous stringers, holding joint fit-up and arc length constant; compare heat input and bead profile. | Two labeled samples plus a parameter log; coach puddle observation and steady travel. | 2, 5, 6; GTAW 16–22, 78–88 |
| 4 | Demonstrate compatible filler identification, feed angle, and timing on clean ferrous coupons. | Add filler to established puddles; alternate short coached passes and independent repetition, stopping on contamination. | One filler-metal coupon with settings and a marked correction; separate torch control from filler timing during coaching. | 2, 5, 6; GTAW 65–69, 83–90 |
| 5 | Demonstrate fillet fit-up and tie-in while preserving gas coverage through the end of each weld. | Build fillet welds on approved steel joints; adjust one variable at a time for profile and fusion. | Labeled fillet coupon and instructor notes on tie-in, contamination, and bead consistency. | 1, 2, 5, 6; GTAW 71–90 |
| 6 | Compare ferrous and approved non-ferrous setup requirements, cleaning, current choices, and filler compatibility. | Instructor demonstration first; students perform non-ferrous trials only on available, approved equipment and material. Continue ferrous fillets if that station is unavailable. | Record which metals were practiced, setup checks, and a quality comparison; do not claim non-ferrous proficiency from observation alone. | 1, 2, 5, 6; GTAW 59–80, 89–93 |
| 7 | Model correction of tungsten inclusion, shielding loss, poor fit-up, and excessive heat. | Diagnose an actual coupon, choose one corrective adjustment, rerun on matched material, and repeat the required ferrous/non-ferrous applications. | Before/after labeled coupons and parameter log; instructor verifies the cause and corrective action. | 1, 2, 5, 6; GTAW 77–93 |
| 8 | Review safety/setup, autogenous and filler-metal work; demonstrate the practical assessment sequence. | Complete GTAW skill checks on available approved ferrous and non-ferrous joints; provide targeted supervised retry time and GMAW transition orientation. | Record process-specific skills and missing evidence; keep the syllabus GTAW category at 35%, without inventing a passing threshold. | 1, 2, 5, 6; GTAW 94–100 |
