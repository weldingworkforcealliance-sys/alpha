# WLD 114 Night Level 1 — 22 day CNC plasma guide draft

**Status:** implementation draft based on the supplied 2026–27 WLD-114 curriculum committee syllabus and WLD-CNC-P instructor planner. WLD 114 meets **5:00–6:00 PM Monday–Thursday, November 9–December 19, 2026**, excluding November 25–29. The 22 actual class dates end December 17. The syllabus controls protected outcomes and grading; the 24-day instructor planner supplies teaching ideas adapted to the actual 22 meetings.

**Protected outcomes from supplied syllabus:** CLO 1 safe Torchmate 4X00 operation; CLO 2 FlexCut 80 consumables and settings; CLO 3 VMD SP2 operation; CLO 4 CAD files and GM output; CLO 5 cut-quality diagnosis and correction; CLO 6 maintenance; CLO 7 CNC-cut project and dimensional accuracy. The JSON draft preserves their exact syllabus wording and links each day to applicable CLO codes.

**Syllabus grading:** Safety & Machine Operation Assessments 20%; Torchmate Academy Quizzes 25%; Torchmate CAD Project Series 25%; Machine Operation Practical Exam 15%; Final CNC-Cut Capstone Project 15%. Students do not have personal Academy access, so instructors must present the licensed Academy videos and quizzes to the class, collect individual answers and evidence in LTG, and avoid claiming that students completed Academy accounts or certificates. Confirm this instructor-administered quiz method with the curriculum owner before final grading setup. The syllabus also mentions an AWS Online Safety Certificate, which is not established by the current instructor-only Academy arrangement; verify how that certificate will be supplied before marking it complete.

**Pair:** WLD 114 is the first class and attendance-primary side of the WLD 114/115 Night Level 1 pair. The WLD 115 shop class follows from 6:00–9:45 PM on each of the same 22 dates. The pair counts as one instructional day per date, but uses separate course guides and grade records.

**Equipment and Academy access:** the installed table is a **Torchmate 4800** with a **FlexCut 80** plasma power source. Confirm the installed torch, consumables and software revision before choosing a cut chart or air requirement. Select cutting amperage and matching consumables from the FlexCut 80 operator manual and approved cut chart for the actual material and thickness; the model's 80 A rating is not a universal cutting setting. The school-provided [Lincoln Electric Learning Torchmate 4000 learning plan](https://lincolnelectric.docebosaas.com/learn/learning-plans/87/torchmate-4000-learning-plan) is the authorized entry point for the named modules below. Signed-in access confirmed the seven-course learning plan and the 4000 Machine Operation video titles; lesson completion and exact video procedures have not been independently verified. The source planner names additional project lessons, which must be matched to the Academy before assigning them.

**Every day:** **5:00–6:00 PM is a full 60-minute WLD 114 Academy block**, with no fixed cleanup period. Only the instructor has Torchmate Academy access. The instructor plays the assigned videos for the class, pauses them for students to follow the procedures in CAD or at the machine, and checks understanding, file readiness and safe equipment use. Students do not need individual Academy logins. The videos provide the lesson sequence; no separate lecture or demonstration block is budgeted. File/part labeling, machine status and safe shutdown occur when the work requires them, before the transition to WLD 115. No student starts a cut until the instructor approves the file, path, material, settings and clear work envelope. The [Torchmate operator hub](../public/resources/wld114/torchmate-operator-hub.html) is a reference for troubleshooting and manufacturer links; Academy videos stay in the school's authorized account.

**Three required project windows:** (1) nameplate **Days 5–7**, (2) welcome sign **Days 7–9**, (3) capstone **Days 9–15**, all due by Day 15 (December 7). Overlap means the next design begins while approved cuts for the previous project rotate through the table. Vectorization, dimensional drawing, kerf/offsets and array/badge/nesting remain **required skills and evidence inside the three projects**, rather than three additional finished pieces. For example, the welcome sign can include a traced element and dimensional verification, while the capstone can require a small nested batch. The source planner listed these as separate activities; this grouping is an implementation proposal pending the approved WLD 114 syllabus, not a change to protected outcomes.

**Planning budget for Days 1–15:** 15 × 60 = **900 minutes for watching and following the Academy lessons**. CAD design, file preparation, project checks and instructor sign-off happen while students follow the relevant videos. The three approved project cuts can rotate during the paired **WLD 115 6:00–9:45 PM lab**: the instructor calls one student to the CNC station while classmates continue assigned welding. Record the CNC artifact against WLD 114 and the welding activity against WLD 115; attendance, grades and protected outcomes stay course-specific. The WLD 115 instructor must supervise/approve each cut, preserve welding instruction and shop oversight, and pause cutting if adequate supervision is unavailable. Reserve cutting slots per student and stage stock in advance. Never record an uncut project as completed.

**Full Academy completion checkpoint:** The signed-in learning plan lists **69 lessons across seven mandatory courses**, targeted for completion by Day 15. Across 900 minutes, this allows an average of about **13 minutes per lesson including pauses and follow-along work**. Two measured video run times are 3:48 and 2:32; the full 69-lesson run time and hands-on pace remain unverified. Use the instructor Academy account to track which videos have been played for the class, and use LTG to record each student’s participation, CAD evidence and three project results. The instructor account’s completion status does not certify an individual student. If the class falls behind, adjust the in-class video sequence; do not assume students can view videos independently or claim an unplayed video was taught.
